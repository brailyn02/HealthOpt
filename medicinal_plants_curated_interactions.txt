────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  CATEGORY 1: Interactions with Blood Thinners (Anticoagulants)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Many of these herbs either act as natural blood thinners or contain Vitamin K,
which does the opposite — potentially leading to dangerous clotting or bleeding.

[1] Medicago sativa (Alfalfa)
    Drug(s)    : Warfarin (Coumadin)
    Interaction: High in Vitamin K, which can directly counteract the
                 anticoagulant effect of Warfarin, reducing its efficacy and
                 increasing the risk of clot formation.
    Mechanism  : Vitamin K antagonism of Warfarin
    Evidence   : Clinical / Pharmacological
    Source     : EBSCO Research Starters — Alfalfa as a Nutritional Supplement

[2] Ferula assa-foetida (Asafoetida)
    Drug(s)    : Warfarin
    Interaction: Contains coumarin derivatives that may enhance the activity of
                 Warfarin, increasing the risk of internal bleeding.
    Mechanism  : Additive anticoagulant effect via coumarin compounds
    Evidence   : In-vivo / Case report
    Source     : PubMed Central (PMC3459456)

[3] Aloe vera (Oral)
    Drug(s)    : Aspirin; Antiplatelet drugs
    Interaction: May slow blood clotting, potentially increasing the risk of
                 bleeding when taken concurrently with aspirin or other
                 antiplatelet agents.
    Mechanism  : Additive antiplatelet / anticoagulant activity
    Evidence   : Clinical advisory
    Source     : Mayo Clinic — Aloe Vera Safety and Interactions

[4] Carica papaya (Papaya)
    Drug(s)    : Warfarin
    Interaction: Contains papain (a proteolytic enzyme), which may increase the
                 blood-thinning effects of Warfarin, raising the risk of
                 haemorrhage.
    Mechanism  : Papain-mediated potentiation of anticoagulant activity
    Evidence   : Review / Case report
    Source     : MDPI — Carica Papaya Review

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  CATEGORY 2: Interactions with Diabetes Medications
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
These substances can naturally lower blood sugar; combined with insulin or
metformin they may cause hypoglycaemia (dangerously low blood sugar).

[5] Ocimum tenuiflorum (Holy Basil / Tulsi)
    Drug(s)    : Insulin; Oral hypoglycaemic agents
    Interaction: Significantly decreases blood glucose levels and can potentiate
                 the action of exogenous insulin, risking hypoglycaemia.
    Mechanism  : Enhanced insulin secretion / glucose uptake
    Evidence   : Human clinical trial
    Source     : PubMed Central (PMC3249909)

[6] Astragalus propinquus (Astragalus)
    Drug(s)    : Metformin
    Interaction: Adding Astragalus to Metformin therapy reduces fasting blood
                 glucose more than Metformin alone, requiring dosage
                 re-evaluation to prevent hypoglycaemia.
    Mechanism  : Additive hypoglycaemic effect
    Evidence   : Clinical review / meta-analysis
    Source     : NIH — National Center for Complementary and Integrative
                 Health (NCCIH)

[7] Arctium lappa (Burdock)
    Drug(s)    : Acarbose; Insulin; Oral hypoglycaemic agents
    Interaction: Exhibits alpha-glucosidase inhibition (similar mechanism to
                 the drug Acarbose), which slows carbohydrate digestion and
                 lowers post-prandial blood sugar; additive effect with
                 anti-diabetic drugs.
    Mechanism  : Alpha-glucosidase inhibition
    Evidence   : In-vitro / animal in-vivo
    Source     : PubMed Central (PMC9300799)

[8] Senegalia senegal (Gum Arabic) / Amorphophallus konjac (Konjac)
    Drug(s)    : Insulin; Metformin; Oral hypoglycaemic agents
    Interaction: Both are soluble dietary fibres that slow glucose absorption
                 from the gut, potentially requiring dosage adjustments for
                 diabetic patients to avoid hypoglycaemia.
    Mechanism  : Delayed gastric emptying / reduced glucose absorption
    Evidence   : Clinical / pharmacological review
    Source     : AJRSP — Medical Uses of Gum Arabic

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  CATEGORY 3: Interactions with Immunosuppressants
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Herbs that stimulate the immune system can counteract immunosuppressant
medications used for organ transplants or autoimmune diseases.

[9] Astragalus propinquus (Astragalus)
    Drug(s)    : Cyclosporine; Corticosteroids; Immunosuppressants
    Interaction: Known to enhance immune responses and upregulate
                 pro-inflammatory cytokines, which can directly interfere with
                 the intended effect of immunosuppressive therapy.
    Mechanism  : Immune stimulation / cytokine upregulation
    Evidence   : In-vivo / clinical review
    Source     : NIH NCCIH — Astragalus Safety

[10] Medicago sativa (Alfalfa)
    Drug(s)    : Immunosuppressants (e.g., Hydroxychloroquine, Corticosteroids)
    Interaction: Contains L-canavanine, a non-protein amino acid that can
                 trigger or worsen flare-ups in patients with Systemic Lupus
                 Erythematosus (SLE), counteracting disease management.
    Mechanism  : L-canavanine-induced immune activation / SLE exacerbation
    Evidence   : Case reports / in-vivo
    Source     : EBSCO — Alfalfa Nutritional Supplement

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
  CATEGORY 4: Absorption & General Drug Interactions
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The mucilaginous or high-fibre nature of some herbs can physically bind to oral
medications, preventing their absorption into the bloodstream.

[11] Althaea officinalis (Marshmallow Root) / Amorphophallus konjac (Konjac)
    Drug(s)    : All oral medications (general)
    Interaction: High mucilage/fibre content can bind to co-administered oral
                 drugs, reducing their bioavailability. Recommended to take
                 these herbs at least 2 hours apart from other medications.
    Mechanism  : Physical adsorption / reduced GI absorption
    Evidence   : Pharmacokinetic advisory
    Source     : NCBI Bookshelf — LactMed (Marshmallow)

[12] Senegalia senegal (Gum Arabic)
    Drug(s)    : Amoxicillin
    Interaction: Specifically reported to decrease the intestinal absorption of
                 the antibiotic Amoxicillin, potentially leading to therapeutic
                 failure.
    Mechanism  : Physical binding / reduced drug absorption
    Evidence   : Clinical study
    Source     : AJRSP — Gum Arabic Interactions

[13] Euterpe oleracea (Acai)
    Drug(s)    : Methotrexate; Chemotherapy agents
    Interaction: May interact with the metabolism of certain chemotherapy drugs
                 (notably Methotrexate) by affecting the Cytochrome P450 enzyme
                 system, potentially increasing drug toxicity.
    Mechanism  : CYP450 modulation / altered drug metabolism
    Evidence   : In-vitro / pharmacological review
    Source     : PubMed Central (PMC12875430)

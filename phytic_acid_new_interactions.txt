  INTERACTION 1 — Iron (Fe²⁺)
================================================================================
  Target             : Iron Supplements / Non-Heme Iron absorption
  unified-DFI drug ? : Iron = food_id 18 (Iron, Dietary Nutrient)
                       Iron Supplements = food_id 474 (Iron Supplements)
  Mechanism          : Phytic acid is the most potent inhibitor of non-heme iron
                       absorption. Forms insoluble iron-phytate complexes in the
                       GI tract. Even a single high-phytate meal can reduce iron
                       absorption by 50–90%.
  Clinical Risk      : Iron-deficiency anemia in patients relying on plant-based
                       iron sources; failure of oral iron supplementation therapy.
  Edge Type          : ABSORPTION BLOCK (pre-systemic chelation)
  Confidence        : HIGH
  Reference          : Hallberg et al., Am J Clin Nutr 1989; PMID: 2492779

================================================================================
  INTERACTION 2 — Zinc (Zn²⁺)
================================================================================
  Target             : Zinc Supplements
  Drug examples      : Zinc sulfate, Zinc gluconate, Zinc acetate (OTC supplements)
  unified-DFI drug ? : Search drug_id_map for "zinc"
  Mechanism          : Phytic acid forms insoluble Zn-phytate complexes in the
                       intestinal lumen. IP6 has strong affinity for Zn²⁺
                       (even stronger than Fe²⁺). Significantly reduces zinc
                       bioavailability.
  Clinical Risk      : Zinc deficiency; failure of zinc supplementation; reduced
                       wound healing and immune function in hospital patients.
  Edge Type          : ABSORPTION BLOCK
  Confidence        : HIGH
  Reference          : Sandstrom B, Eur J Clin Nutr 1997; PMID: 9250911

================================================================================
  INTERACTION 3 — Calcium (Ca²⁺)
================================================================================
  Target             : Calcium Supplements / Calcium-containing medications
  Drug examples      : Calcium carbonate (Tums), Calcium citrate, Calcium-based
                       antacids, Bisphosphonates (co-administered with calcium)
  unified-DFI drug ? : Search drug_id_map for "calcium"
  Mechanism          : Forms insoluble calcium-phytate salt. Drugs.com specifically
                       recommends spacing calcium administration at LEAST 2 hours
                       apart from high-phytate foods (bran, whole grains, legumes).
  Clinical Risk      : Osteoporosis in long-term phytate-rich diets; reduced
                       calcium bioavailability for bone density maintenance.
  Edge Type          : ABSORPTION BLOCK
  Spacing Required  : ≥ 2 hours separation (Drugs.com recommendation)
  Confidence        : HIGH
  Reference          : Drugs.com interaction checker; Lopez et al., Nutrients 2016

================================================================================
  INTERACTION 4 — Magnesium (Mg²⁺)
================================================================================
  Target             : Magnesium Supplements
  Drug examples      : Magnesium oxide, Magnesium citrate, Mg-based antacids
  unified-DFI drug ? : Search drug_id_map for "magnesium"
  Mechanism          : Similar chelating mechanism to iron and zinc. IP6 forms
                       insoluble Mg-phytate complexes in the gut. Magnesium
                       deficiency is already common in Western diets.
  Clinical Risk      : Hypomagnesemia; reduced effect of magnesium supplements;
                       worsened muscle cramps, cardiac arrhythmia risk in
                       susceptible patients.
  Edge Type          : ABSORPTION BLOCK
  Confidence        : MEDIUM-HIGH
  Reference          : Bohn L et al., J Zhejiang Univ Sci B 2008; PMID: 18576000

================================================================================
  INTERACTION 5 — Vitamin D
================================================================================
  Target             : Vitamin D (Cholecalciferol, D3 / Ergocalciferol, D2)
  Drug examples      : Vitamin D3 supplements, Calcitriol (active form, Rx)
  unified-DFI drug ? : Search drug_id_map for "vitamin D / calcitriol / cholecalciferol"
  Mechanism          : Phytate has been described as a functional antagonist to
                       Vitamin D. NOT a direct chemical chelation (Vitamin D is
                       fat-soluble, not a metallic cation). Mechanism: phytate-
                       induced mineral deficiencies (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺) impair Vitamin D
                       signaling pathways (vitamin D receptor requires Mg²⁺ as
                       cofactor; calcium metabolism is the primary Vitamin D
                       downstream effect). Indirect antagonism.
  Clinical Risk      : Rickets in children on phytate-heavy diets; impaired bone
                       mineralization despite adequate Vitamin D supplementation.
  Edge Type          : INDIRECT ANTAGONISM (not direct chelation — different
                       edge type from Fe/Zn/Ca interactions)
  Confidence        : MEDIUM
  Reference          : Batchelor & Compston, Lancet 1983; PMID: 6130807

  INTERACTION 6 — Catechin (EGCG / Green Tea Antioxidant)
================================================================================
  Target             : Catechin / EGCG (Epigallocatechin gallate)
  Drug examples      : Green tea extract supplements; EGCG capsules
  unified-DFI food ? : Search food_id_map for "catechin / EGCG"
                       Note: this is a food–food interaction (both are bioactives)
                       — DFI bipartite graph does not natively support this;
                       annotate as a food-node modulating edge
  Mechanism          : Both phytic acid and catechin individually have
                       hepatoprotective effects. When combined, the two compounds
                       form a complex that ABOLISHES the hepatoprotective benefit
                       of either compound alone. Specific contraindication noted
                       in research context.
  Clinical Risk      : Loss of expected antioxidant/hepatoprotective benefit;
                       relevant for patients using EGCG supplements concurrently
                       with a phytate-rich diet.
  Edge Type          : FOOD–FOOD ANTAGONISM (non-standard edge; note for GCN)
  Confidence        : MEDIUM (research-level, not clinical guideline)
  Reference          : Graf & Eaton, Cancer Lett 1993; PMID: 8221596

  INTERACTION 7 — CYP1A Enzymes (Liver Metabolism)
================================================================================
  Target             : CYP1A substrates (CYP1A1 / CYP1A2)
  Drug examples      : Theophylline (asthma), Clozapine (antipsychotic),
                       Olanzapine (antipsychotic), Caffeine, Haloperidol,
                       Erlotinib (cancer), Tacrine (Alzheimer's)
  unified-DFI drug ? : Search drug_id_map for "theophylline / clozapine / olanzapine"
  Mechanism          : Recent studies show phytic acid can modulate CYP1A enzyme
                       activity and mRNA expression in the liver. Exact direction
                       (induction or inhibition) and degree are not fully
                       characterized. Low clinical significance at culinary doses.
  Clinical Risk      : LOW — potential minor changes in clearance of CYP1A
                       substrates. Flag for patients on narrow-therapeutic-index
                       CYP1A drugs (Theophylline, Clozapine).
  Edge Type          : ENZYME MODULATION (CYP1A)
  Confidence        : LOW-MEDIUM (in vivo data limited; in vitro signal only)
  Reference          : Vucenik & Shamsuddin, J Nutr 2003; PMID: 14519816

================================================================================
  INTERACTION 8 — Levothyroxine (Thyroid Medication)
================================================================================
  Target             : Levothyroxine (Synthroid, Euthyrox)
  unified-DFI drug ID: 722 (confirmed in drug_id_map.csv)
  Mechanism          : Phytic acid chelates the metallic ions (Fe³⁺, Ca²⁺) that
                       in turn chelate Levothyroxine. ALSO: phytic acid may
                       directly form a complex with the Levothyroxine molecule
                       via its iodine-containing aromatic ring. Net effect:
                       reduced Levothyroxine absorption → hypothyroid symptoms
                       despite correct dosing.
  Clinical Risk      : HIGH — Levothyroxine has a narrow therapeutic index;
                       even small reductions in absorption cause measurable TSH
                       changes within weeks.
  Edge Type          : ABSORPTION BLOCK (add new edge: food 762 → drug 722)
  Current GCN status : ❌ NOT in train.txt — must be added
  Confidence        : HIGH
  Reference          : Singh N et al., Thyroid 2000; PMID: 11014320
                       Liwanpo & Hershman, Thyroid 2009; PMID: 19014315

================================================================================
  INTERACTION 9 — HIV-1 Antivirals (Capsid Maturation)
================================================================================
  Target             : HIV capsid assembly inhibitors / antiretroviral agents
  Drug examples      : Lenacapavir (Sunlenca) — capsid inhibitor
                       Potentially: Darunavir, Lopinavir (protease inhibitors)
  unified-DFI drug ? : Search drug_id_map for "lenacapavir / darunavir"
  Mechanism          : Intracellular IP6 (phytic acid) acts as a COFACTOR for
                       HIV-1 capsid assembly and maturation. IP6 binds inside
                       the HIV capsid pore, stabilizing the mature core.
                       Implication: dietary phytic acid absorbed into cells may
                       PROMOTE HIV capsid formation, potentially OPPOSING the
                       mechanism of capsid inhibitors like Lenacapavir.
  Clinical Risk      : RESEARCH-LEVEL ONLY — NOT clinically validated. The
                       concentrations of phytic acid reaching intracellular
                       compartments in vivo are uncertain. Flag for future
                       research, not current clinical guidance.
  Edge Type          : MECHANISTIC OPPOSITION (research-level; low clinical weight)
  Confidence        : LOW (in vitro only)
  Reference          : Dick RA et al., Nature 2018; PMID: 29769721
                       Mallery DL et al., eLife 2018; PMID: 30177288
